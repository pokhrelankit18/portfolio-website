Padel Match Analytics

Turning ordinary padel match footage into the things players and coaches actually ask about: who hit what, where everyone stood, and which points broke the rules.

Ankit Pokhrel
2 min read·2026

What the client wanted

Padel is filmed constantly, but almost nobody watches the footage twice. The client wanted that footage to become something useful without a person sitting through every rally with a notepad. Upload a match, get back the answers a coach or player would ask for: how many shots each person played, what kind, where they spent the match, how rallies started and ended, and when a point broke the rules. It had to run on their own machines, with their own models, and give results they could trust enough to show to paying players.

What we delivered

A backend the client runs themselves. They hand it a match video and get a folder back, named by date and match, with a short note explaining every file inside. For each match it produces:

  • An annotated match video. Every player keeps the same ID for the whole match, with rackets, body pose, court zones and walls drawn in, and each hit labelled with the shot type.
  • A clean tracking replay. The match redrawn on a plain court with stick-figure players and the ball's path, easy to read on a phone and free of the camera's clutter.
  • Shot by shot data. Who hit the ball, when and with which shot (serve, forehand, backhand, smash, bandeja or lob), ready to open in a spreadsheet or feed into an app.
  • Rallies and rule calls. Where each point started and finished, plus padel-specific faults such as a serve into the fence or a ball hitting the glass before the floor.
  • Charts. A heatmap of where each player stood, ball speed over the match and each player's mix of shots.

How it gets used

  • Players see their own match in numbers: how often they smash, how much they lean on the backhand, and whether they really stay at the net as much as they think.
  • Coaches jump straight to the moments worth talking about, such as every serve or every lost point, instead of scrubbing through an hour of video. Heatmaps make positioning problems obvious at a glance.
  • Clubs and the client's app get structured data for every match, so they can track a player over a season, compare players, or build highlight reels automatically.

Making it trustworthy

The hardest part was making the numbers believable. In an early client demo the system counted hits that never happened. When I went through it frame by frame, more than a third of the hits it reported were the ball simply flying past a racket. I rebuilt the hit detection to look for the ball actually changing direction at a racket, then checked it against a minute of play I had labelled by hand. It now catches 78% of real hits, and on the far side of the court, where players look tiny, it finds 7 of 9 hits instead of 1.

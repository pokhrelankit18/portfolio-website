SPYDER: One Photo, Hours of Footage

Find a person in low-resolution surveillance video from a single photo, and say "no match" honestly instead of always guessing.

Ankit Pokhrel
1 min read·2026·write-up on medium

The problem

On clean photos face recognition is close to solved: NIST reports error rates around 0.15% for the best systems. Surveillance footage isn't clean. Faces are small, blurred, compressed, dark and turned away, and the same models fall hard there (one drops from 65.2% to 29.9% rank-1 on real CCTV). A system that always returns its closest guess will hand an investigator the wrong person.

What I built

A complete pipeline: video in, faces detected (SCRFD), tracked, filtered by a quality gate, aligned and embedded, then matched.

  • Live Watch: armed subjects are matched in real time and raise one alert per appearance, with evidence.
  • Recorded Search: each video gets a cached FAISS index, and one photo returns ranked candidate tracks or "no match".
  • Three embedding models behind one interface (ArcFace R50, SFace, AdaFace IR-101), so they can be compared under identical conditions.
  • A calibrated threshold set from strangers' scores at a target false-alarm rate, so "no match" is a real answer.
  • 31 REST endpoints plus a WebSocket event channel, 63 automated backend tests, and CI.

Built to produce leads, not verdicts

Every candidate is marked unverified and needs a human to hold-to-confirm or reject. No raw live video is stored, only alert evidence (the best face and one frame), and expired data is garbage-collected. Volunteers gave written consent, and their data stays on the collecting machine.

Fast enough for a laptop

On a MacBook Air M2 with CoreML, face detection takes about 18 ms per frame and each embedding 3-5 ms. Indexing recorded video at 5 sampled frames per second runs about 7.5x faster than real time, so an hour of footage takes roughly 8 minutes, and searching a video's faces takes about 0.04 ms per query.

Status

All software milestones are built and tested. What's left is the evaluation itself: recording the consented volunteer gallery and running the degradation, calibration and subgroup experiments.
